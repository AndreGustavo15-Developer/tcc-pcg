Procedural content generation is an approach used in dgital game development to 
automatically produce lements such as environments, maps, and levels. However, 
the use of different generation strategies does not guarantee that the 
resulting content will be completable, diverse, or suitable for the player
experience. In this context, this research project aims to comparatively 
analyze three procedural level generation strategies for 2D platform games:
random generation, rule-based generation, and genetic algorithm-based 
generation. The study is classified as applied, exploratory, descriptive, and 
experimental research, adopting a mixed-methods approach that combines 
quantitative and qualitative procedures. To conduct the study, a 2D platform
game prototype will be developed, in which the three strategies will share the 
same computational level representation, physics model, and evaluation 
criteria. Level completability will be investigated using an automated 
procedure based on Breadth-First Search (BFS), while structural diversity and
computational performance will be assessed through quantitative metrics. 
An exploratory evaluation involving players is also planned, using gameplay
sessions and questionnaires to investigate aspects of the player experience, 
including perceived difficulty, enjoyment, satisfaction, and variety. The study 
is expected to identify differences among the strategies regarding level 
completability, structural diversity, computational cost, and participant 
experience, contributing to a better understanding of the characteristics and 
limitations of different procedural level generation approaches for 2D platform 
games.
